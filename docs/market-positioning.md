# AI Triad Research: Market Positioning

Geoffrey Moore positioning statements grounded in actual product capabilities.

---

## Elevator Pitch

AI Triad Research is a multi-perspective research platform that structures AI policy debates through a curated taxonomy of 1,197 nodes (785 POV nodes across three philosophical camps — Accelerationist, Safetyist, Skeptic — plus 412 shared situation nodes), using formal argumentation frameworks to quantify argument strength, track convergence, and surface the cruxes that actually divide competing worldviews. Unlike general-purpose AI chat tools that produce single-perspective responses, it forces genuine multi-perspective engagement grounded in scholarly literature and produces auditable, quantified analysis rather than opaque summaries.

## Competitive Landscape

### General-Purpose AI Chat (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini)

**What they do well:** Broad knowledge, fast responses, accessible interface, general-purpose reasoning.

**Where they fall short for this use case:** Produce single-perspective responses that reflect training data consensus. Cannot maintain three distinct philosophical positions simultaneously. No formal argumentation structure. No convergence tracking. No taxonomy grounding. Responses are opaque — no way to audit which arguments support or attack which claims.

**AI Triad differentiation:** Forces genuine multi-perspective engagement through separate character agents with distinct BDI profiles. Every claim is grounded in a curated taxonomy node. Argument strength is computed formally (QBAF/DF-QuAD), not asserted. Neutral evaluator provides bias detection layer that general chat tools lack entirely.

### AI Safety Forums (LessWrong, Alignment Forum)

**What they do well:** Deep technical discourse, expert community, canonical reference posts.

**Where they fall short:** Self-selecting community creates perspective blindness. No structured opposition — disagreement is organic and inconsistent. No formal argument tracking. Discussion threads are unstructured and difficult to synthesize.

**AI Triad differentiation:** Structural guarantee of multi-perspective engagement (all three camps are always represented). Steelman validation ensures opposing positions are faithfully represented, not straw-manned. Convergence signals detect echo-chamber dynamics (argument recycling, position accommodation without genuine concession).

### Structured Argumentation Tools (Kialo, DebateGraph)

**What they do well:** Visual argument mapping, pro/con structure, community contribution.

**Where they fall short:** Manual argument entry — no AI-driven debate generation. No quantified argument strength. No convergence tracking. No audience-aware framing. Static maps rather than dynamic debates.

**AI Triad differentiation:** AI-driven debate with four-stage turn pipeline (BRIEF-PLAN-DRAFT-CITE). Quantified argument strength via QBAF (not just binary pro/con). Adaptive phase transitions driven by convergence signals. Audience-aware rhetoric (same topic, different framing for different stakeholders). Calibration system auto-tunes debate parameters across 100+ runs.

### Policy Research Databases (SSRN, Google Scholar, think tank publications)

**What they do well:** Comprehensive literature access, citation tracking, peer review quality signal.

**Where they fall short:** Individual papers present single perspectives. Cross-paper synthesis is manual. No structured way to identify where papers agree vs. disagree vs. talk past each other.

**AI Triad differentiation:** 75+ PowerShell cmdlets for document ingestion with confidence-gated extraction (FIRE pipeline). Taxonomy-grounded summarization maps source claims onto the multi-perspective BDI framework. Conflict detection across the taxonomy surfaces inter-perspective tensions that manual review misses. Situation nodes reveal where authors use the same terms with different meanings.

### Academic Debate Platforms (Oxford-style, Intelligence Squared)

**What they do well:** High-production-value adversarial discourse, audience engagement, entertainment value.

**Where they fall short:** Binary framing (for/against). No computational analysis. No crux tracking. No argument network. Outcomes are audience votes, not analytical synthesis.

**AI Triad differentiation:** Three-perspective framework avoids false binary framing. Computational analysis (QBAF, convergence signals) supplements rhetorical assessment. Crux tracking identifies the actual disagreements, not just the loudest ones. Neutral evaluator provides analytical assessment independent of rhetorical performance.

## Differentiation Pillars

### 1. Multi-Perspective BDI Taxonomy Grounding

Every debate is grounded in a curated taxonomy of 320+ nodes across three philosophical camps (Accelerationist, Safetyist, Skeptic), each decomposed into Beliefs (empirical claims), Desires (normative commitments), and Intentions (strategic proposals). This structural decomposition prevents the collapse of empirical and normative disagreements into a single dimension — a fundamental limitation of flat stance-detection approaches and general-purpose AI chat.

**Evidence:** 785 POV nodes, 412 situation nodes with per-camp BDI interpretations, 500+ typed edges, 384-dimensional embeddings, 1,091+ policy actions in shared registry.

### 2. Formal Argumentation with Quantified Strength

Argument strength is computed using QBAF (Quantitative Bipolar Argumentation Framework) with DF-QuAD gradual semantics, not asserted or voted on. Attack relationships are typed (rebut, undercut, undermine) with distinct weights. BDI-aware base score calibration reveals asymmetries in AI's ability to evaluate different claim types — a finding invisible to systems without formal argumentation.

**Evidence:** `argumentNetwork.ts` implements DF-QuAD; `calibrationLogger.ts` tracks 9+ parameters across debate runs; BDI sub-scoring with evidence quality, falsifiability, and mechanism specificity dimensions.

### 3. Convergence-Driven Adaptive Debate

Phase transitions are not fixed by round number but driven by seven deterministic convergence signals: argument redundancy, dialectical engagement, move polarity, position drift, concession tracking, crux resolution, and pragmatic convergence. When signals indicate diminishing returns, the engine triggers synthesis rather than continuing unproductive rounds.

**Evidence:** `convergenceSignals.ts` implements all seven signals; `phaseTransitions.ts` uses adaptive staging with configurable exit thresholds; calibration optimizer auto-tunes thresholds from empirical debate data.

### 4. Bias Detection Through Neutral Evaluation

An independent evaluator assesses debates with all persona information stripped (Speaker A/B/C instead of character names, no taxonomy context, no persona descriptions). Speaker assignment is randomized per debate to prevent positional bias. Users see both the persona-grounded view and the neutral view — divergence surfaces systematic bias that would be invisible in a single-perspective system.

**Evidence:** `neutralEvaluator.ts` with three evaluation checkpoints (baseline, midpoint, final); randomized speaker mapping; crux classification as empirical/values/definitional; claim assessment status tracking (well_supported through refuted).

### 5. Audience-Aware Multi-Format Debate

The same topic produces substantively different debates depending on the target audience (policymakers, technical researchers, industry leaders, academics, general public) and debate protocol (Structured, Socratic, Deliberation). Each audience gets custom writing style directives, argument structure templates, moderator behavior bias, and taxonomy node prioritization — not just tone adjustment, but different evidence standards and rhetorical strategies.

**Evidence:** Five audience presets with full prompt customization; three debate protocols with distinct action sets and moderator strategies; per-audience BDI category weighting for taxonomy node selection.
