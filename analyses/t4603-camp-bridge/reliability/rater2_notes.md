## Rubric ambiguities

1. **Node-title valence is often undefined.** Several nodes (e.g. "Legal and Regulatory Frameworks for AI Accountability," "Judicial Mandate of Operational Product Design Remedies") read as neutral/descriptive titles rather than a stated directional claim, so "same direction" (rule for genuine) can't always be checked against the title alone — I had to infer an implied camp-typical direction, which is itself a judgment call the rubric doesn't instruct how to make.
2. **"Topic match only" vs. "wrong scope" vs. "misread" overlap heavily** when a document uses a non-AI example (crisis communications, austerity, genome biology-as-fact, historical psy-ops) to illustrate a pattern the node states in AI-specific terms. The rubric's wrong-scope example (non-AI system / hardware bias) covers some of these but doesn't clearly settle whether a *generalizable mechanism* argument from a non-AI domain counts as wrong-scope-stretched or as genuine-by-analogy. I defaulted to stretched whenever the cited passage itself wasn't about AI systems.
3. **Bridge-inference test ("delete the sentence and see if it still supports") is hard to apply mechanically** when the inserted sentence ("this aligns with X concerns," "safetyists would interpret this as...") is doing real synthesis work rather than pure assertion — in several items (e.g. r1 C39, r2 C42) the remaining content was *partially* supportive, just not as directly as the point claims, which the binary delete-test doesn't cleanly resolve into stretched vs. genuine.
